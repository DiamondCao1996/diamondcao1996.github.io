About

Understanding how we work
with emerging technologies.

I am a doctoral student at Chalmers University of Technology, researching cognitive augmentation in production systems. I study how virtual reality, social robots and digital twins can support people in manufacturing.

My work combines human-centered technology evaluation, study design and interaction prototyping, with attention to mental workload, spatial cognition and task performance. My broader interests include affective computing and exploratory applications of AI in health management.

Exploratory research / 2026–present · In development

MOND: physiological trends & everyday reflection

How might personal physiological trends and self-reported feelings support everyday reflection, particularly for neurodivergent people? MOND explores this question through a companion concept combining optical pulse sensing, feeling notes and use context, with an ear-clip audio concept and a digital companion interface.

Built artefacts include browser interface previews and the “My nights” sample-data research preview of overnight pulse variability, resting heart rate, sleep and activity forecasts with illustrative uncertainty. MOND Rest Lab provides a browser field guide for resting-baseline collection and a proposed participant-paced VR safety experiment; the page itself does not collect sensor data.

The project is in development. Sensing, comfort and wellbeing support still need evaluation. The night preview is not a validated measure of sleep quality, recovery, stress or health; the VR process remains a research concept.

Hardware collaborator: Dr. Liangyu Chen
